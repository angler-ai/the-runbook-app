Chat apps have no shell, so they use your server's remote MCP endpoint
(`/mcp`) instead of the CLI. Two things to add, each once:

- **The connector**: your server's `/mcp` URL. You sign in with OAuth, and
  the app gets the [MCP tools](/reference/mcp/) with the scopes you tick.
- **The skill**: `therunbook-skill.zip` from the [latest
  release](https://github.com/angler-ai/the-runbook-app/releases/latest/download/therunbook-skill.zip).
  It teaches the model the agent loop, when to ask a person, and how to
  write runbooks.

What works well from a chat: asking what a run is waiting on, finding past
runs and memory, drafting and validating a runbook, and working an agent
step with you watching. Long unattended work belongs in an agent with a
shell or a [launcher](/agents/custom/#start-agents-from-a-webhook).

<Aside type="caution" title="The server must be reachable from the internet">
Claude and ChatGPT connect from their own servers, not from your machine,
so `https://localhost` or an address on a private network won't work. Use
the URL your browser opens the web app at, if it's public.
</Aside>

## Claude

Works in Claude on the web, Claude Desktop and the mobile apps (one setup,
on your account). Custom connectors and skills are available on Claude's
paid plans; on Team and Enterprise an owner may need to allow them first.

<Steps>

1. **Add the connector.** In Claude, open **Settings → Connectors**, choose
   **Add custom connector**, name it `The Runbook` and enter
   `https://runbooks.example.com/mcp`. Choose **Connect**: your server's
   sign-in page opens. Sign in, tick the scopes Claude may use
   (`runbooks:publish` stays off unless you tick it) and approve.

2. **Upload the skill.** Download
   [`therunbook-skill.zip`](https://github.com/angler-ai/the-runbook-app/releases/latest/download/therunbook-skill.zip),
   then in **Settings → Capabilities → Skills** choose **Upload skill** and
   pick the zip. On Team and Enterprise plans an owner can upload it once
   in the organization settings, so everyone gets it.

3. **Try it** in a new chat with the connector turned on:

   ```text
   What is run exe_01JD2X6PZ5R8E2W4Y6A0C3D5F7 waiting on?
   ```

</Steps>

Claude's help center covers [skills](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512180)
and custom connectors in detail.

## ChatGPT

ChatGPT connects to MCP servers in **developer mode**, and reads skills in
the same folder format as Claude. Which plans have each feature, and
whether a workspace admin has to turn it on first, changes often: check
OpenAI's [developer mode article](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461)
and [skills guide](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/build-skills).

<Steps>

1. **Turn on developer mode** in ChatGPT's settings (under the apps or
   connectors section's advanced settings). On Business, Enterprise and Edu
   workspaces an admin enables it for the workspace.

2. **Create the connector** with the URL
   `https://runbooks.example.com/mcp` and OAuth authentication. ChatGPT
   registers itself with your server; sign in and tick the scopes it may
   use.

3. **Upload the skill** where your plan offers skills, using
   [`therunbook-skill.zip`](https://github.com/angler-ai/the-runbook-app/releases/latest/download/therunbook-skill.zip).
   Without skills on your plan, the connector still works: the tools
   describe themselves, and the [prompt library](/agents/prompts/) has
   prompts to paste.

4. **Try it** in a chat with the connector selected.

</Steps>

ChatGPT's deep research and company knowledge use the connector's read-only
`search` and `fetch` tools to find and read runbooks, runs and active
memory across projects the grant can read. `search` returns up to 20
matches with IDs and links. `fetch` reads one by ID. See the
[MCP reference](/reference/mcp/#search).

<Aside type="note" title="Write actions ask first">
ChatGPT asks you to confirm a tool call that changes something, such as
claiming or reporting a step, unless you allow it for the conversation.
</Aside>

## What the connector can and can't do

The connector is an [OAuth grant](/agents/#credentials-the-agent-is-never-a-person):
the server records it as an agent you sponsor, with only the scopes you
ticked. It can claim and report agent steps, search runs and memory, and
draft and validate runbooks. It can never decide a human approval, even
though you signed it in.

See and disconnect connected apps with `runbook apps list` and
`runbook apps revoke <client>`, or remove the connector in the app.
