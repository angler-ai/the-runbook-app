People are told about work three ways: the web app's inbox, email, and
Slack. Webhooks reach your own systems the same way.

## Email

Approvals, assignments and handoffs, reminders, escalations and failed runs
go by email to people with a verified address, as text and HTML, with a link
into the app and a one-click unsubscribe. Each person picks `all`,
`approvals_only`, `daily_summary` or `none` in their account settings.
Email uses the same SMTP settings as sign-in codes (`RUNBOOK_SMTP_URL`,
`RUNBOOK_SMTP_FROM`). Mail goes through a durable outbox: a failed send is
retried and never holds up a run.

## Slack

A project can post its approvals, handoffs, reminders and failed runs to a
Slack channel, two ways:

| | Incoming webhook | The Runbook's Slack app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set up by | A project admin | The operator once, then project admins |
| Messages | A line linking to the run | The same, with a button per approval option |
| Decide from Slack | No | Yes, as the person who clicks. Destructive approvals open in the web app |

### Incoming webhook

In **Project settings → Slack notifications**, paste an incoming-webhook
URL (`https://hooks.slack.com/…`). It's stored and never shown again.

### The Slack app

<Steps>

1. **Create the app** from
   [`deploy/slack/manifest.yaml`](https://github.com/angler-ai/the-runbook-app/blob/main/deploy/slack/manifest.yaml):
   at api.slack.com/apps choose **Create New App → From a manifest**, after
   replacing `runbook.example.com` with your host, so Slack sends clicks to
   `https://<host>/v1/slack/interactions`. Install it to your workspace.

2. **Give the server its secrets:**

   | Docker/Kubernetes | Cloudflare | |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `RUNBOOK_SLACK_BOT_TOKEN` | the same, as a secret | The Bot User OAuth Token (`xoxb-…`) |
   | `RUNBOOK_SLACK_SIGNING_SECRET` | the same, as a secret | **Basic Information → Signing Secret** |
   | `RUNBOOK_SLACK_DEFAULT_CHANNEL` | the same, as a var | Optional: a channel ID for projects that name none |

   Both secrets or neither. With the GKE Terraform module, use
   `slack_bot_token`, `slack_signing_secret` and `slack_default_channel`.

3. **Pick each project's channel.** Invite the app to the channel
   (`/invite @The Runbook`), then enter the channel's ID (`C…` or `G…`) in
   **Project settings → Slack notifications**.

</Steps>

### How a click becomes a decision

- Each click is checked against Slack's signature, made with your signing
  secret; requests more than five minutes old are refused.
- The person who clicked is looked up in Slack and matched to a Runbook
  account **by email**. That account must be an active member of the
  organization.
- If a decision can lead to a destructive step, Slack links back to the
  run so the person can decide there.
- Other decisions go through the normal decision route with that person's
  own permissions: approver rules, minimums and one vote per person apply
  exactly as in the app. The credential used lasts one minute and is revoked
  straight away.
- The person gets a reply only they can see, such as "Your vote (approve)
  is recorded." or "You are not an approver for this step."

<Aside type="note">
Slack is best effort. Email, webhooks and the inbox stay the record, and a
Slack outage never holds up a run.
</Aside>
